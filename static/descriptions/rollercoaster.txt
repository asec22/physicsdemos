<p style="text-align: justify;">Note that the mass <math><mi>m</mi></math> cancels — the speed depends only on the <i>height difference</i>, not the mass of the cart. The cart can only reach a hill if that hill's height is less than the initial height <math><msub><mi>h</mi><mn>0</mn></msub></math>. If a hill exceeds <math><msub><mi>h</mi><mn>0</mn></msub></math>, the cart will stop before reaching the peak.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;">Some questions to consider while viewing the demonstration:</p>
<ul>
<li>What happens to the cart's speed at the bottom of a valley compared to the top of a hill?</li>
<li>Set a hill height equal to the initial height. Does the cart make it over? What does this reveal about energy conservation?</li>
<li>Since mass cancels out, would a heavier cart travel the same path at the same speed? Explain why or why not in a real-world scenario.</li>
<li>Identify where on the track the cart has maximum kinetic energy and maximum potential energy.</li>
</ul>
