<p style="text-align: justify;"> In this demonstration, we observe an ideal fluid flowing through a pipe that narrows at its midsection (a <i>Venturi tube</i>). Two fundamental principles govern the flow: the <b>Continuity Equation</b> and <b>Bernoulli's Equation</b>.</p>

<p style="text-align: justify;"><i>Continuity Equation</i> &mdash; for an incompressible fluid the volumetric flow rate is constant:</p>

<math display="block">
    <mrow>
        <msub><mi>A</mi><mn>1</mn></msub>
        <msub><mi>v</mi><mn>1</mn></msub>
        <mo>=</mo>
        <msub><mi>A</mi><mn>2</mn></msub>
        <msub><mi>v</mi><mn>2</mn></msub>
    </mrow>
</math>

<p style="text-align: justify;">So the fluid speeds up when the pipe narrows:</p>

<math display="block">
    <mrow>
        <msub><mi>v</mi><mn>2</mn></msub>
        <mo>=</mo>
        <mfrac>
            <mrow><msub><mi>A</mi><mn>1</mn></msub><msub><mi>v</mi><mn>1</mn></msub></mrow>
            <msub><mi>A</mi><mn>2</mn></msub>
        </mfrac>
    </mrow>
</math>

<p style="text-align: justify;">Because speed increases in the throat, pressure <i>decreases</i> there &mdash; this is the <b>Venturi effect</b>. The animation shows particles (water, <math><mrow><mi>&rho;</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>1000</mn></mrow></math> kg/m&sup3;) flowing through the pipe and bunching closer together in the narrow section, illustrating the increase in speed. The title displays the computed inlet velocity, throat velocity, and pressure difference.</p>
